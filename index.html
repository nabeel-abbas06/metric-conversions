Introduction

The metric system originated in France in 1799 following the French Revolution although decimal units had been used in many other countries and cultures previously. Although there have been many different measurements and the definitions of the units have been revised, the official system of measurements of most countries is the modern form of the metric system which is known as the "International System of Units".

Since other systems of measurement are still used around the world, such as the United States and the United Kingdom, this site aims to help people convert units of measurement with metric converters and metric conversion tables and to better understand alternative measurements that they are unfamiliar with:

Length

Length is the measurement of how long something is from one end to the other.

Examples:
  • Length of a table
  • Length of a road
  • Length of a rope
Units:

Feet (ft), Inches (in), Meters (m), Millimeters (mm), Yards (yd), Miles (mi), Kilometers (km)

Area

Area is the amount of surface covered by a flat shape.

Examples:
  • Classroom floor
  • Garden land
  • Table top
Units:

mm², in², ft², yd², m², mi², km², Acres, Hectares

Volume

Volume is the amount of space an object occupies or can hold. You can think of it as the capacity of an object for liquids, solids, or gases.

Examples:
  • Water bottle
  • Box space
  • Swimming pool
  • Liquid container in kitchen
Units:

fl oz, mL, gal, L, ft³, m³, yd³

Mass

Mass is the amount of matter in an object and does not change with location or gravity. It is a measure of how much substance an object contains.

Examples:
  • Human body
  • School bag
  • Apple
  • Stone
Units:

Ounces (oz), Grams (g), Pounds (lb), Kilograms (kg), Short Tons, Metric Tons

Temperature

Temperature measures how hot or cold something is and indicates the level of heat energy.

Examples:
  • Ice temperature
  • Boiling water
  • Human body
  • Room temperature
Units:

Celsius (°C), Fahrenheit (°F), Kelvin (K)
