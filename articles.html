Q1. What is the SI System of Units?

The SI system of units is the internationally agreed system for measuring physical quantities. It provides a standard and consistent way to measure length, mass, time, temperature, and more.

It is the modern form of the metric system and is used worldwide in science, engineering, medicine, and trade.

Quantity SI Unit Symbol
Length metre m
Mass kilogram kg
Time second s
Temperature kelvin K
Electric current ampere A
Amount of substance mole mol
Luminous intensity candela cd

Q2. Differences Between Metric Systems

a. Original Metric System

Developed in France (1795). Base units included meter, kilogram, liter, and second.

b. CGS System

Base units: centimeter, gram, second. Used mainly in early physics research.

c. MKS System

Base units: meter, kilogram, second. Used in engineering applications.

d. SI System

The modern international standard with seven fundamental units.

Q3. Definition of the Meter

a. Original Definition

Defined as one ten-millionth of the distance from the equator to the North Pole.

b. Platinum-Iridium Bar

In 1889, a physical platinum-iridium bar stored in France defined the meter.

c. Wavelength of Light

In 1960, the meter was defined using wavelengths of krypton-86 light.

d. Current Definition

Since 1983, the meter is defined as the distance light travels in vacuum in 1/299,792,458 seconds.

Q4. Traditional Units Used in the United States

a. Length

Unit Relationship
Inch 12 inches = 1 foot
Foot 3 feet = 1 yard
Yard 36 inches = 1 yard
Mile 5,280 feet = 1 mile

b. Weight

Unit Relationship
Ounce 16 ounces = 1 pound
Pound 2,000 pounds = 1 ton

c. Volume

Unit Relationship
Cup 2 cups = 1 pint
Pint 2 pints = 1 quart
Quart 4 quarts = 1 gallon

Conclusion

Although the U.S. still uses customary units, science and international trade depend mainly on the metric system.
